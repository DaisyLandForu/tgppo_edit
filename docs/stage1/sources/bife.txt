Figure 1: The overall framework of BiFE. To balance search efficiency and rule quality, FHF evaluates heuristics
via expensive MILP solving while FLF offers a cheap surrogate by measuring consistency with strong-branching
decisions. An LLM generator produces new heuristics through three operations: initialization, crossover, and
mutation. The evolutionary process has two stages: (1) Initialization: the LLM generates an initial population,
ranked by FLF with the top α forming the elite set E for FHF evaluation. (2) Evolutionary Loop: parent selection is
elite-prioritized, where elites are ranked by FHF and others by FLF , and selected parents are passed to the LLM for
crossover or mutation. New candidates with FLF exceeding threshold τ undergo FHF evaluation and are admitted
into E. Survival selection retains all elites and fills the remaining slots from the general population. This selective
allocation lets BiFE explore broadly with FLF while using FHF to correct the surrogate on promising candidates.

F.3   Illustrative examples
On standard benchmarks, the rules discovered by
BiFE and Symb4CO exhibit clear differences in
complexity. The rules found by BiFE are shown
in Listing 2 (CA), Listing 3 (SC), Listing 4 (MIS)
and Listing 5 (CFL). For comparison, the rules
discovered by Symb4CO on the same benchmarks
are provided in Listing 6, Listing 7, Listing 8, List-
ing 9. Even from a purely visual inspection, BiFE’s
rules exhibit markedly more sophisticated structure
than those of Symb4CO. Taking the rules discov-
ered on CA as an example, BiFE’s rule (Listing 2)
first extracts and caps several features, computes
a harmonic mean of pseudocosts, combines them
with a weighted sum via a geometric mean, and fi-
nally applies a logistic transformation to spread the
scores—demonstrating a clear, multi-step algorith-
mic logic. In contrast, Symb4CO’s rule (Listing 6)
simply multiplies a sequence of input features to-
gether in a single expression, with no intermediate
reasoning or non-linear processing. This qualitative
contrast aligns with the quantitative comparison in
Figure 5, suggesting that LLM-based methods in-
herently generate more complex algorithmic logic
suited for challenging MILPs.

 3 def candidate_variable_score(variable_features) -> float:
 4     """
 5     Assign a score to this candidate variable reflecting the potential dual bound
       improvement resulting from branching on it.
 6
 7
 8    Args:
 9    variable_features: Feature vector of the candidate variable, 72 dimensions.
10
11    Return:
12    A score indicating how much the dual bound would improve if this variable were
      chosen as the branching variable.
13    """
14    f = variable_features
15    frac = max(f[18], 1e-10)
16    up_pc = max(f[20], 1e-10)
17    down_pc = max(f[21], 1e-10)
18    harmonic_pc = 2.0 / (1.0/up_pc + 1.0/down_pc)
19    weighted_active_sum = max(f[67], 1e-10)
20
21    geom_part = (frac * harmonic_pc) ** 0.5
22    logit_input = geom_part * weighted_active_sum
23    # Inverse logistic to center and spread scores
24    raw = logit_input / (logit_input + 1.0)
25    return 1.0 / (1.0 + np.exp(-5.0 * (raw - 0.5)))

 3 def candidate_variable_score(variable_features) -> float:
 4     """
 5     Assign a score to this candidate variable reflecting the potential dual bound
       improvement resulting from branching on it.
 6
 7
 8    Args:
 9    variable_features: Feature vector of the candidate variable, 72 dimensions.
10
11    Return:
12    A score indicating how much the dual bound would improve if this variable were
      chosen as the branching variable.
13    """
14    import math
15
16    fractionality = variable_features[18] # feature 19
17    up_pseudocost = variable_features[20] # feature 21
18    down_pseudocost = variable_features[21] # feature 22
19    weighted_active_count = variable_features[66] # feature 67
20    unweighted_active_count = variable_features[48] # feature 49
21
22    # Geometric mean of pseudocosts
23    if up_pseudocost <= 0 or down_pseudocost <= 0:
24        pseudocost_geom = 0.0
25    else:
26        pseudocost_geom = math.sqrt(up_pseudocost * down_pseudocost)
27
28    # Weighted harmonic mean of active constraint counts (avoid division by zero)
29    if weighted_active_count > 0 and unweighted_active_count > 0:
30        harmonic_mean = 2.0 / (1.0 / weighted_active_count + 1.0 /
      unweighted_active_count)
31    else:
32        harmonic_mean = max(weighted_active_count, unweighted_active_count)
33
34    # Product with fractionality, then log-scale clamp
35    raw_score = fractionality * pseudocost_geom * (1.0 + harmonic_mean)
36
37    # Normalize via log transformation and clamp to [0, 1]
38    if raw_score <= 0:
39        return 0.0
40    normalized = math.log(1.0 + raw_score) / math.log(1.0 + 100.0)
41    return min(1.0, normalized)

 3 def candidate_variable_score(variable_features) -> float:
 4     """
 5     Assign a score to this candidate variable reflecting the potential dual bound
       improvement resulting from branching on it.
 6
 7
 8    Args:
 9    variable_features: Feature vector of the candidate variable, 72 dimensions.
10
11    Return:
12    A score indicating how much the dual bound would improve if this variable were
      chosen as the branching variable.
13    """
14    frac = variable_features[18]
15    up_pseudo = variable_features[20]
16    down_pseudo = variable_features[21]
17    pseudo_sum = variable_features[23]
18    active_count = variable_features[48]
19    sum_abs_dual = variable_features[67]
20
21    # Fractionality component: tanh scaling
22    frac_score = math.tanh(frac * 3.0)
23
24    # Pseudocost component: geometric mean normalized by (1 + sum)^0.5
25    if up_pseudo > 1e-9 and down_pseudo > 1e-9:
26        geo_mean = math.sqrt(up_pseudo * down_pseudo)
27    else:
28        geo_mean = max(up_pseudo, down_pseudo)
29    pseudo_score = geo_mean / (1.0 + math.sqrt(pseudo_sum))
30
31    # Activity component: product of active count and dual-weighted sum, log-scaled
32    if active_count > 0 and sum_abs_dual > 0:
33        activity_score = math.log1p(active_count * sum_abs_dual)
34    else:
35        activity_score = 0.0
36
37    # Weighted sum and compress with square root
38    score = (2.5 * frac_score + 3.5 * pseudo_score + 2.0 * activity_score)
39    return math.sqrt(max(score, 0.0))
